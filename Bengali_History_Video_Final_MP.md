# MASTER PROMPT: DAILY BENGALI HISTORY VIDEO

Create one engaging, historically accurate Bengali documentary video about a qualifying historical event. The complete exported video must last **50–60 seconds**, including narration, pauses, opening text, transitions, and the closing frame.

Every selected event must have happened **at least 100 years before the intended publication date**. Exclude subjects centred on anyone's birth or death. Events **more than 150 years old** may be selected independently of today's month and day and may use a source-supported approximate historical date when an exact date is unavailable.

Use the rules below to research, select, script, produce, and verify the result. These eligibility and accuracy rules take precedence over topic preferences, visual appeal, and the requirement to produce a daily episode.

## 1. PRODUCTION SETTINGS

Use these defaults unless I provide replacements:

- **Publication date:** Today's date, determined when this prompt runs. Use a supplied intended publication date instead when provided.
- **Timezone:** Europe/London.
- **Spoken language:** Clear, accessible standard Bengali throughout.
- **Audience:** General Bengali-speaking YouTube viewers.
- **Duration:** 50–60 seconds; aim for approximately 57 seconds.
- **Default format:** Standard YouTube landscape video, 16:9, 1920 × 1080.
- **If I specify YouTube Shorts:** Use 9:16, 1080 × 1920, while retaining the same 50–60 second duration.
- **Voice:** Male narrator with a warm, pleasant, medium-deep voice and calm documentary delivery.
- **Pronunciation:** Apply the mandatory speech overrides in Section 10: **গৃহীত → গৃহীতো (Grihito)** and **সংখ্যালঘু → শংখা লঘু** whenever those words occur.
- **Narration subtitles:** **OFF by default.** Do not burn Bengali narration subtitles or their background bands into the video, include a subtitle stream, or supply an automatically loaded subtitle sidecar. Keep concise scene titles, dates, graphic labels, and reconstruction disclosures. Add narration subtitles only if I explicitly request them.
- **Output goal:** A finished video plus its production package, when the necessary tools are available.
- **Repeat policy:** Do not reuse a historical event already recorded in the supplied or accessible publication log unless I explicitly permit a repeat.

Record the publication date separately from the event's historical date. Daily publication does not by itself establish a historical anniversary.

## 2. APPLY THE EVENT-AGE RULES

Calculate age using calendar dates relative to the intended publication date, not simply the difference between year numbers.

### Events less than 100 years old

Reject them without exception.

### Events 100–150 years old, inclusive

Require a verified exact historical date matching the publication month's name/number and day.

This is the default editorial policy retained from the original “On This Day” concept. An event exactly 100 years old is eligible. An event exactly 150 years old remains in this band.

### Events more than 150 years old

The historical month and day do not have to match the publication month and day.

- Retain an exact historical date when reliable sources establish one.
- When the exact date is unavailable, use the best precision supported by the sources: month/year, year, circa year, or a documented period or range.
- Make approximate dating explicit; never fabricate a day, month, year, or degree of certainty.

For example, if publication is on 11 September 2026, the minimum-age cutoff is 11 September 1926. The flexible selection exception applies only to events before 11 September 1876.

For uncertain dates, assess the latest plausible date in the supported interval. The entire interval must establish eligibility. Use the approximate-date exception only when the entire interval is more than 150 years old. Reject candidates whose age or applicable date rule cannot be resolved.

For events spanning several dates, select one clearly defined milestone or accurately describe the bounded event interval. Do not use the start of a long process to make later developments appear old enough.

## 3. EXCLUDE BIRTHS, DEATHS, AND BIOGRAPHICAL ANNIVERSARIES

Do not select:

- Anyone's birth or birth anniversary.
- Anyone's death or death anniversary.
- Assassinations, executions, funerals, or memorial occasions centred on a person's death.
- Biographies or general life stories presented as the episode's central subject.
- Later commemorations used as substitutes for the date of the original historical event.

People may appear as participants in an otherwise eligible event, such as signing a treaty, leading an expedition, passing a reform, or demonstrating an invention.

A battle, uprising, or other collective event may qualify when the story concerns what happened and its consequences. Mention casualties only when relevant and verified; do not turn the episode into an individual's death-anniversary story.

## 4. SELECT A DISTINCT HISTORICAL EVENT

Focus on one identifiable occurrence or milestone that can be understood within one minute.

Suitable categories include:

- Battles, military campaigns with a clearly bounded milestone, and peace agreements.
- Treaties, laws, reforms, constitutional changes, and institutional decisions.
- Revolutions, independence movements, public demonstrations, and social reform milestones.
- Bengal, Indian, South Asian, British Empire, and wider world history.
- Institutional founding, infrastructure openings, and important public works.
- Documented scientific discoveries, invention milestones, and public demonstrations.
- Exploration milestones and significant voyages or arrivals.
- Archaeological discoveries, cultural milestones, and historically significant publications.

All categories remain subject to the age, date, evidence, and birth/death exclusions. No topic category overrides those requirements.

For discoveries and inventions, identify the specific milestone supported by the evidence, such as a demonstration, patent, publication, or operational use. Do not confuse these dates or make unsupported “first ever” claims.

## 5. RESEARCH AND BUILD A SHORTLIST

First check for eligible events matching the publication month and day. Also research eligible events more than 150 years old without requiring a calendar-day match.

Aim to identify 3–5 plausible candidates, but do not invent or include unsuitable candidates to reach that number. A smaller verified shortlist is acceptable.

For each candidate, record:

- Event name and location.
- Historical date or supported period.
- Date precision and relevant uncertainty.
- Whether it satisfies the age and date rules.
- Whether it is a verified same-day anniversary.
- Whether the central subject passes the birth/death exclusion.
- Why it is historically significant and suitable for a short video.
- Whether it has already appeared in the accessible publication log.

Use actual research tools when available. Do not claim to have searched, opened sources, or verified facts if those actions were not performed.

## 6. VERIFY THE SELECTED EVENT

Require at least **two credible, independent sources** supporting the core event and its chronology. Two websites repeating the same account do not provide independent confirmation.

Prefer archives, museums, universities, scholarly histories, established reference works, and relevant primary documents. Use primary material where useful, with scholarly context when interpretation or dating is disputed. Treat unsourced anniversary lists and search snippets as discovery aids.

Verify:

1. What actually happened.
2. When it happened and how precise that date is.
3. Where it happened, using historically appropriate place names.
4. Who participated, where relevant.
5. Essential background.
6. Immediate outcome and well-supported historical significance.

Provide an evidence table linking the important claims to their supporting sources, with source title, institution/author, URL or precise bibliographic reference, and a brief supporting note.

Do not invent quotations, dialogue, statistics, causes, people, locations, dates, or visual details presented as established facts. Use numerical claims only when necessary and supported. If credible sources disagree, disclose the uncertainty or omit the disputed detail.

Preserve relevant calendar conventions. Do not claim a modern calendar-day anniversary unless the date correspondence is established. Do not convert an uncertain or disputed historical date into a confident anniversary.

If essential facts cannot be verified, choose another candidate. If no qualifying event can be verified, report that limitation instead of fabricating an episode or relaxing the rules.

## 7. CHECK PREVIOUS EPISODES AND CHOOSE ONE EVENT

Consult the supplied or accessible publication log before making the final selection. Identify repeats by the underlying event, including alternative spellings and translated names.

After eligibility, evidence, and repeat checks pass, rank candidates by:

- Historical importance.
- Clarity of the story within one minute.
- Relevance and interest for Bengali-speaking viewers.
- Strength of the available evidence.
- Opportunities for accurate, understandable visuals.

Choose one event automatically. Do not ask me to select among qualifying candidates.

If no log is available, state that repeat checking was unavailable and provide a record for future use. Do not imply knowledge of earlier episodes that you cannot access.

## 8. USE TRUTHFUL SERIES BRANDING

Use anniversary wording only when the event's exact month and day match publication and that match has been verified.

**Verified same-day episode:**

- Bengali series label: “আজকের দিনে ইতিহাস”
- English series label: “ON THIS DAY IN HISTORY”
- “আজকের এই দিনে” may be used naturally in narration.

**Episode without a verified same-day match:**

- Bengali series label: “ইতিহাসের এক অধ্যায়”
- English series label: “A CHAPTER IN HISTORY”
- Introduce the actual historical date or period directly.
- Do not use “আজকের এই দিনে,” “On This Day,” “Today in History,” or equivalent anniversary claims in narration, titles, closing cards, descriptions, or hashtags.

For approximate dates, use wording such as “আনুমানিক [বছর] সালে,” “[শতক]-এর দিকে,” or a source-supported date range. Match the wording to the actual evidence. A documented year alone does not automatically mean the year itself is uncertain.

Display the historical date or period clearly. Do not place the publication date beside the event in a way that implies an unverified anniversary.

## 9. BUILD A 50–60 SECOND STORY

Use this approximately 57-second structure as the default:

| Time | Purpose | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 0–3 seconds | Hook | A specific, accurate question, tension, or surprising fact about the selected event. |
| 3–9 seconds | Event identification | What happened, where, and the verified date or supported period. |
| 9–17 seconds | Background | The minimum context needed to understand the event. |
| 17–35 seconds | Main event | Explain the central action or turning point clearly. |
| 35–49 seconds | Result | Explain the immediate outcome and its consequences. |
| 49–57 seconds | Lasting significance | One memorable, evidence-based takeaway and a brief closing frame. |

Adjust timings to fit the actual narration while keeping the complete export within 50–60 seconds. Include transitions, pauses, and the closing frame within those timings.

Use one central story, a small number of essential facts, and only the names needed for understanding. Avoid long lists and generic introductions. Separate immediate results from lasting significance so the ending does not repeat the previous section.

Do not sacrifice accuracy for a dramatic hook or claim that every event “changed the whole world.”

## 10. WRITE AND TIME THE BENGALI VOICEOVER

Write the entire spoken script in natural, accessible Bengali. Use Bengali pronunciations for names and terms, with pronunciation notes outside the spoken script when helpful.

**Bengali narration verb-ending preference (mandatory):**

- Use these preferred spoken forms: **দিল → দিলো; গেল → গেলো; করল → করলো; হয়েছিল → হয়েছিলো; এল → এলো; পেল → পেলো; হল → হলো**.
- Write the preferred forms in the complete voiceover, scene-by-scene narration, and speech-generation input. Pronounce the final **ও** sound clearly and naturally rather than ending abruptly at **ল**.
- Apply the same preference to comparable verb endings where grammatically appropriate. Do not change nouns such as **খাল**, proper names, or unrelated words simply because they end in **ল**.
- Preserve these requested narration forms during proofreading.

**Specific Bengali pronunciation overrides (mandatory):**

| Standard written word | Required spoken pronunciation / phonetic speech input |
|---|---|
| গৃহীত | **গৃহীতো — Grihito**, with a clear, natural final **ও** sound; do not end at “Grihit”. |
| সংখ্যালঘু | **শংখা লঘু**, articulated as these two connected sound groups at a natural pace. |

- Apply these overrides whenever the corresponding words occur in narration. Do not insert either word merely to demonstrate its pronunciation.
- Keep the standard spellings **গৃহীত** and **সংখ্যালঘু** in the written script, scene titles, graphic labels, description, and other displayed or published text. The phonetic forms above are speech instructions, not replacement spellings for those materials.
- Supply the exact speech-generation input separately from the standard written script, with the pronunciation mappings recorded. Use the required phonetic forms in the actual TTS input, or an equivalent supported pronunciation control that produces the requested sounds. Give the same pronunciation directions to a human narrator.
- Preserve the mappings after script edits and timing revisions. Reuse cached narration only when its actual speech input and pronunciation settings match the final version; regenerate any affected segment after a pronunciation change.
- Listen to the affected words in the generated narration and final mix where tools permit. Check the final **ও** in **গৃহীতো** and the natural articulation of **শংখা লঘু**. A saved phonetic input alone does not prove the audio pronunciation is correct; report explicitly if listening verification could not be performed.

Voice direction:

- Warm, pleasant male voice with a medium-deep pitch.
- Calm documentary presentation and natural emotional expression.
- Clear standard Bengali pronunciation.
- Comfortable speaking speed with short pauses after important names, dates, and ideas.
- No rushed, robotic, exaggerated, or theatrical delivery.

Start with approximately **100–120 Bengali words as a drafting estimate**. Word count is not proof of runtime. Generate or record the chosen narration, measure it, and revise the wording until it fits comfortably within the planned timeline.

For a 57-second edit, aim to finish narration by approximately 55 seconds, leaving a brief closing frame. Avoid accelerating the voice unnaturally to force a fit.

Provide both the complete written voiceover and its scene-by-scene segments. They must contain the same final wording. The separately labelled speech-generation input may differ only through documented phonetic pronunciation overrides, without changing the meaning. Proofread Bengali spelling, grammar, names, dates, and meaning after any timing revision.

If audio cannot be generated or measured, label the timing as an estimate and leave runtime verification explicitly incomplete.

## 11. CREATE A SCENE-BY-SCENE PRODUCTION PLAN

For each scene, provide:

- Scene number, start time, end time, and duration.
- Exact Bengali narration segment.
- Visual action and historically appropriate setting.
- Camera framing and movement.
- On-screen title, date, graphic-label, and disclosure placement. Record **“Narration subtitles: off”** unless I explicitly request subtitles; specify their placement only when requested.
- Music, ambience, and sound effects.
- Transition to the next scene.
- Whether the visual is authentic source material, a map/graphic, or a reconstruction.

Split longer scenes into shorter shots when the generation tool requires it. Keep their combined duration within the scene allocation. Do not add unplanned time when assembling shots.

## 12. CREATE VISUAL AND VIDEO-GENERATION PROMPTS

Provide a separate, usable prompt for every generated scene or shot. Include the event period, location, subject, action, environment, clothing, equipment, lighting, composition, camera movement, duration, and output aspect ratio.

Maintain continuity in people, costumes, weather, geography, and objects across shots. Use the same references where the tools support them.

Preferred visual style:

- Cinematic historical documentary.
- Realistic lighting and restrained colour treatment.
- Period-appropriate settings and props.
- Smooth camera movement and purposeful close-ups.
- Clear maps and diagrams when they explain the event better than reconstruction.
- Archival texture only where appropriate; never imply that generated imagery is authentic footage.

Do not rely on image/video generation to render final Bengali text, factual newspaper headlines, maps, or document quotations accurately. Add verified text and graphic overlays during editing.

Instruct visual/video-generation tools not to add narration subtitles or subtitle background bands by default. Keep the scene titles and explanatory/disclosure labels specified in the production plan.

## 13. CHECK HISTORICAL VISUAL ACCURACY

Check each scene against its specific date and location, including:

- Clothing, uniforms, weapons, tools, and transport.
- Architecture and landscape.
- Flags, symbols, maps, and territorial boundaries.
- Available technology and forms of communication.

Avoid anachronisms, fantasy elements, implausible explosions, and graphic violence. Do not impose a blanket absence of technology; show only technology appropriate to the event's period.

Do not invent a supposedly authentic newspaper front page, portrait, document, or quote. Use clearly illustrative graphics when an authentic source is unavailable. Distinguish verified details from plausible reconstruction.

Label reconstructed scenes clearly, for example “ঐতিহাসিক পুনর্নির্মাণ” / “Historical reconstruction.” Identify photorealistic AI-generated visuals and include the appropriate YouTube upload disclosure instruction under the platform's current requirements. An on-screen disclosure label does not replace an applicable platform disclosure.

## 14. ADD READABLE ON-SCREEN TEXT — NO DEFAULT NARRATION SUBTITLES

Use Bengali as the primary on-screen language. Add concise English translations for key titles, event names, and dates where useful.

- Keep titles short and readable on a phone.
- Use high contrast and a font that correctly renders Bengali characters.
- Keep essential text clear of interface overlays and the edges of the chosen format.
- **Do not display Bengali narration subtitles during playback by default.** Do not replace them with full English narration subtitles.
- Do not draw narration caption text or its background band into any scene. Do not include an embedded subtitle stream or a default/forced subtitle track in the export.
- Do not create or bundle an SRT, VTT, or other subtitle sidecar by default; this also avoids automatic loading by a player. Retain measured narration/scene timings as production data instead.
- Keep concise Bengali/English scene titles, historical dates, names, map/diagram labels, and archive/reconstruction disclosures. These are on-screen labels, not full narration subtitles.
- Display approximate date labels consistently in both languages.

Supply the exact text for every title card and overlay. For the default delivery, state **“Narration subtitles: off; no subtitle track or sidecar supplied.”**

Only if I explicitly request subtitles, add the requested language and delivery format. Align them to final measured narration, keep them readable, and avoid simultaneous full Bengali and English subtitles. Provide an SRT or other sidecar only when requested; if audio is unavailable, mark its timings as provisional. A request for a separate subtitle file alone does not authorise burning captions into the video or enabling them by default.

## 15. ADD MUSIC AND SOUND EFFECTS

Use music that supports the event's tone without overwhelming the voice:

- Opening: Restrained curiosity or tension.
- Main event: Appropriate documentary momentum.
- Ending: Reflective or resolved, according to the historical outcome.

Use historically appropriate ambience and sound effects only when they serve the scene. Keep narration intelligible, lower music under speech, avoid clipping, and check the mix on ordinary speakers or headphones.

Use original, licensed, or verified public-domain assets. Record the source, reuse permission, and required credit for external visuals, music, and effects. If asset permission cannot be established, replace the asset. Do not assume an asset is reusable simply because its subject is old.

## 16. RENDER AND VERIFY THE VIDEO

When the necessary tools are available, generate or assemble the visuals, narration, title/graphic/disclosure overlays, music, and sound effects into an actual video. Add narration subtitles only when explicitly requested.

Export an MP4 with H.264 video and AAC audio in the selected dimensions. Do not describe an export as 4K unless its actual resolution is 4K.

Check the completed export for:

- Total runtime of **50–60 seconds**, including the complete opening and ending.
- Correct aspect ratio and resolution.
- Clear Bengali narration, including the requested **গৃহীতো (Grihito)** and **শংখা লঘু** pronunciations wherever those words occur.
- No narration subtitle text or caption background bands visible during default playback; no subtitle stream or automatically loaded sidecar in the default delivery. Confirm this through encoded-frame review and export stream/file inspection.
- Readable scene titles, dates, graphic labels, and disclosure overlays. If subtitles were explicitly requested, verify their requested format, timing, readability, and default-display setting separately.
- Correct historical dates, names, labels, and scene continuity.
- No clipped text, missing shots, unwanted black frames, abrupt audio cuts, or overpowering music.
- Clear distinction between authentic material and reconstructions.
- Consistency between the video and its title, description, and sources.

Watch and listen to the final export where tools permit. Report which checks were actually performed. Revise and recheck any failed requirement.

If video rendering tools are unavailable, return the complete production package with status **“Production package only — video not rendered.”** Identify the remaining production steps. Never describe a script, storyboard, image sequence, or generation prompt as a finished video.

## 17. CREATE THE YOUTUBE TITLE

Provide one concise, compelling Bengali + English title reflecting the selected event.

For a verified anniversary, “আজকের দিনে ইতিহাস” / “On This Day” may be used. For other episodes, use the event name or general history branding without an anniversary claim.

Make the title accurate and specific. Avoid misleading clickbait and unsupported superlatives. Keep any date or period consistent with the research and video.

## 18. CREATE THE DESCRIPTION AND HASHTAGS

Write a brief bilingual Bengali + English description, approximately 2–4 short sentences in total, covering what happened, its historical date or period, and why it matters.

Append a compact source list and any required asset credits or reconstruction/AI disclosure note outside that narrative summary. Clearly identify approximate chronology when applicable.

Provide 5–8 relevant hashtags with the # symbol. Use a mixture of general history, Bengali-language, and event-specific tags.

Use #OnThisDay, #TodayInHistory, or Bengali anniversary equivalents only for a verified same-day episode. For other episodes, use general tags such as #History, #BengaliHistory, and #ইতিহাস, together with accurate event-specific tags.
